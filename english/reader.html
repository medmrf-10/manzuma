The Tail End

The Tail End Life is measured not in days or experiences, but in both the quantity and quality of time you spend with the people you love.

How do you measure a life?

The author shows multiple attempts to quantify the essence of a life.

First, he tries to quantify life in days, weeks, months, and years with charts that show hundreds of dots or squares to measure the units of time.

(Shortform note: The average human lifespan is 79 years, so throughout this summary we'll round up and use an 80-year lifespan as the standard).

But he determines that that measurement still feels incomplete.

The author figures that life is more about experiences than pure, quantifiable time.

So, next, he calculates how many times he'll experience certain events and activities.

He also takes into account how many of those experiences he's already used up in his 34 years, compared to how many he still has left.

Consider these examples, imagining that you're also 34 years old and maintain the same rate of experiences throughout your life: - You have celebrated 34 New Year's Eves and you have 46 left. - If you eat at a restaurant four times a month, you have already enjoyed 1,632 meals and you have 2,208 left. - If you manage to go on a tropical vacation once every five years, you have already enjoyed six vacations and you can look forward to 10 more.

Finally, the author puts things into starker terms as he examines things that don't happen at a consistent rate throughout your life.

Specifically, the time you spend with your parents and siblings peaks during the early years of your life.

By the time you're an adult you're at the tail end of your time together-not the time you're alive simultaneously, but the days you actually spend physically together.

Let's quantify it: - Most kids are with their parents at some point nearly every day until they graduate high school.

If you spent 95 percent of your days with your parents until your 18th birthday, that's 6,241.5 days. - Assume that, after you turn 18, you move to another city for college and then work.

From that point on, you visit your parents three times a year for four days per visit; that equates to 744 days throughout your adult life. - But remember, you're already 34, so you have 552 days left.

That means you've already spent 92 percent of your total days together.

Use Your Remaining Time Wisely With this insight, the author has three suggestions for making the most of the time you have left: 1.

Live near your loved ones.

This is one of the most straightforward ways to spend more days with the people who are important to you.

2.

Prioritize your time.

Be conscious about how you choose to spend your time.

3.

Savor the time you have.

When you're around your loved ones, be present and enjoy that time, because you know how fleeting it is.
